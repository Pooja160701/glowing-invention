# SupplyPulse Positioning & Differentiation

## Positioning statement
For supply-chain teams operating across fragmented ERP, WMS, procurement, and planning systems, SupplyPulse is an operational decision-support platform that turns inventory and supply signals into prioritized, explainable, human-approved actions.

Unlike a broad ERP or planning suite, SupplyPulse is designed as a focused layer across existing systems rather than a replacement for them.

## Differentiation pillars
### 1. Exception-first
Start with the few events that require attention instead of forcing users to scan every dashboard.

### 2. Explainable
Every material recommendation should expose the important inputs, assumptions, changes, and rationale.

### 3. Trusted
Display source, freshness, lineage, and data-quality indicators alongside decision-critical information.

### 4. Human-controlled
Recommendations can be accepted, modified, rejected, or escalated. Material actions remain auditable.

### 5. Connected context
Inventory, demand, PO, supplier, and lead-time signals should appear together when they affect the same exception.

### 6. Closed-loop
Track owner, decision, action, and resolution so the system learns operationally even before advanced ML optimization is introduced.

## What SupplyPulse should not claim
- It should not claim to replace SAP, Oracle, Manhattan, Kinaxis, or Blue Yonder.
- It should not claim superior forecast accuracy without controlled evidence.
- It should not claim autonomous procurement in the MVP.
- It should not imply that AI recommendations are inherently correct.

## Strategic narrative
Broad platforms solve many planning problems. SupplyPulse's hypothesis is that teams can benefit from a narrower layer optimized for operational attention and accountable decisions.
