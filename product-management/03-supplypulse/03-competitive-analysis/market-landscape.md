# SupplyPulse Market Landscape

## 1. Market categories
| Category | Typical scope | Representative platforms |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise supply-chain planning | Demand, supply, inventory, S&OP, scenarios | SAP IBP, Blue Yonder, Kinaxis |
| ERP-integrated inventory planning | Inventory, demand/supply planning, procurement workflows | Oracle NetSuite |
| Unified planning + execution | Planning connected directly to WMS/OMS/TMS/execution | Manhattan Active |
| Specialist inventory optimization | Forecasting, replenishment, service-level optimization | Various specialist vendors |
| Analytics / decision layer | Cross-system visibility and operational analytics | BI + custom platforms |

## 2. Competitive dynamics
### Enterprise suites
Large suites can provide broad process coverage, integration, governance, and advanced planning. Their breadth can also create implementation complexity and a larger change-management footprint.

### ERP-integrated solutions
ERP-native planning can reduce system fragmentation because inventory transactions and planning data share an underlying business system. ## 3. Unmet-need hypotheses
These are hypotheses for SupplyPulse research, not claims that competitors lack these capabilities.
1. Teams may need a lighter operational layer across existing ERP/WMS/planning systems.
2. Exception triage may deserve a first-class workflow rather than being buried inside planning modules.
3. Data freshness and lineage may need to be visible at the moment a decision is made.
4. Recommendation explanations may need to be accessible to non-specialist stakeholders.
5. Human approval and audit history may be valuable between algorithmic recommendation and downstream execution.

## 4. Market-entry implication
SupplyPulse should initially behave like a decision-support and exception-management layer, not an ERP replacement or autonomous procurement system.
