# SupplyPulse Interview Guide

> **Use:** semi-structured discovery interviews.  
> **Portfolio note:** the dataset in this repository is synthetic; this guide is intended for future real-user validation.

## Opening
- Thank the participant.
- Explain that the goal is to understand their current workflow, not sell a product.
- Confirm that no confidential company information is required.
- Ask permission before recording in a real research setting.

## Context
1. What is your role and what supply-chain decisions do you own?
2. What does a typical planning/operations day look like?
3. Which systems do you use most often?

## Inventory workflow
4. How do you determine whether inventory is healthy?
5. Which inventory measures do you check first?
6. Tell me about the last time inventory data looked wrong or inconsistent.
7. What did you do to reconcile it?

## Exception workflow
8. How do stockout risks reach you?
9. How do you distinguish urgent exceptions from routine exceptions?
10. Tell me about the last exception that consumed significant time.
11. What information was missing when you first saw it?

## Replenishment
12. Walk me through the last reorder decision you made.
13. Which inputs mattered most?
14. How do you account for lead time and lead-time variability?
15. Who approves the decision?
16. When would you reject a system recommendation?

## Supplier management
17. How do you monitor supplier performance?
18. How do you discover late or at-risk purchase orders?
19. What supplier information is hardest to obtain quickly?

## Data trust
20. Which data sources do you trust most and least?
21. What makes a number credible enough to act on?
22. How do you identify stale, incomplete, or duplicated data?

## Current tools
23. Which tasks still happen in Excel or spreadsheets?
24. What dashboards do you use?
25. Where do you copy information between systems?

## Future-state exploration
26. What would make a supply-chain decision easier without taking control away from you?
27. What would a useful recommendation need to explain?
28. Which decisions should always require human approval?
29. What would make you stop using a new tool?

## Closing
30. If you could remove one recurring supply-chain task tomorrow, what would it be?
31. What metric would prove that a new solution is helping?
32. Who else should we speak with?

## Interviewer notes
Capture:
- direct behavior,
- exact workflow,
- tools used,
- decision criteria,
- workarounds,
- emotional intensity,
- frequency,
- business impact,
- unresolved questions.

Avoid converting a participant's requested feature directly into a requirement without validating the underlying problem.
