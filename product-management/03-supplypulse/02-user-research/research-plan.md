# SupplyPulse Research Plan

> **Status: SYNTHETIC RESEARCH PLAN** — designed for portfolio demonstration. No real participant research is claimed.

## 1. Research goal
Understand the workflows, pain points, decision criteria, trust requirements, and information gaps that affect inventory and supply-chain decisions.

## 2. Research objectives
| Objective | What we need to learn |
|---|---|
| Inventory visibility | How users establish current inventory health |
| Exception management | How urgent exceptions are detected and prioritized |
| Replenishment | Inputs and approval logic behind reorder decisions |
| Supplier risk | How lead-time and supplier-performance changes are monitored |
| Data trust | Reconciliation steps, source systems, and confidence signals |
| Decision support | What makes a recommendation actionable and explainable |
| Workflow | Handoffs between planning, procurement, warehouse, and leadership |

## 3. Target participants
- Inventory planners responsible for SKU/location inventory.
- Procurement managers responsible for supplier and PO performance.
- Warehouse/operations managers responsible for physical inventory and exceptions.
- Supply-chain leaders responsible for service, working capital, and operational performance.

## 4. Methodology
A mixed qualitative approach is recommended:
1. 45–60 minute semi-structured interviews.
2. Workflow walkthrough using a recent inventory or replenishment decision.
3. Artifact review where participants can safely share non-sensitive examples.
4. Lightweight follow-up survey for prioritization.

For this portfolio, the qualitative evidence is **synthetically generated** to demonstrate the analysis process.

## 5. Sampling framework
Seek variation across:
- company size,
- number of locations,
- SKU count,
- planning maturity,
- ERP/WMS landscape,
- manual vs automated workflows,
- make-to-stock vs other operating models.

## 6. Key research questions
1. Walk me through how you know which SKUs need attention today.
2. What happens when inventory numbers disagree across systems?
3. How do you detect an impending stockout?
4. How do you identify excess or aging inventory?
5. What inputs drive a reorder decision?
6. How do supplier lead-time changes affect planning?
7. What makes you trust or reject a forecast?
8. What would you need to see before approving a recommendation?
9. Which work is still performed in spreadsheets?
10. What operational outcome would make a new platform valuable?

## 7. Success criteria
Research is useful if it produces:
- recurring workflow patterns,
- clearly differentiated user needs,
- evidence-backed problem hypotheses,
- measurable opportunity areas,
- explicit assumptions requiring validation.

## 8. Bias controls
- Do not treat frequency in synthetic data as market prevalence.
- Separate observed behavior from participant interpretation.
- Record contradictory evidence.
- Avoid leading questions.
- Avoid asking participants to design the solution before understanding the problem.
- Validate high-impact hypotheses with real users before implementation.
