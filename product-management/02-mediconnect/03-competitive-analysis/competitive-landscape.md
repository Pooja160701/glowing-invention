# Competitive Landscape

## 1. Market structure

MediConnect sits at the intersection of four product patterns:

1. **Healthcare marketplaces** — discovery + provider profiles + appointment booking.
2. **Practice-management platforms** — schedules, staff workflows, availability, and operational controls.
3. **Patient portals / health-system platforms** — self-service appointments, communications, and longitudinal patient access.
4. **Care-access infrastructure** — APIs/integrations that make scheduling available across multiple digital entry points.

The market is therefore not one homogeneous category. Different competitors optimize different parts of the care-access journey.

## 3. Competitive dimensions

| Dimension | Why it matters to MediConnect |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Helps patients find appropriate providers and locations |
| Availability accuracy | Reduces booking failure and staff intervention |
| Self-service changes | Reduces calls for cancellation/rescheduling |
| Patient communications | Improves preparation and attendance |
| Staff operations | Determines whether the product creates or removes administrative work |
| Scheduling complexity | Matters for multi-provider, multi-location, and sequential visits |
| Integration | Determines whether availability and bookings remain synchronized |
| Privacy/security | Critical for healthcare trust and regulatory obligations |
| Analytics | Enables product and operational improvement |
| Care-journey continuity | Connects booking to preparation and follow-up without entering clinical decision-making |

## 4. Strategic implication

The opportunity is not simply to reproduce online appointment booking. A defensible MediConnect product hypothesis is to connect **patient self-service + reliable availability + operational exception handling + transparent communication + privacy-aware measurement** into one coherent administrative journey.

This is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that MediConnect is superior to the competitors above.
