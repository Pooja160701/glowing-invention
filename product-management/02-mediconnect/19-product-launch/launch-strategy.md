# Launch Strategy

## Launch model

**Controlled B2B2C pilot**

Start with a limited number of participating provider organizations before broader expansion.

## Launch phases

### Phase 0 — Internal readiness
Validate:
- Product.
- Engineering.
- Security/privacy.
- Analytics.
- Support.
- Operations.

### Phase 1 — Internal pilot
Use controlled internal scenarios and test accounts.

Goals:
- Validate workflows.
- Validate monitoring.
- Validate recovery procedures.

### Phase 2 — Limited organization pilot
Introduce a small set of participating organizations.

Goals:
- Validate real operational workflows.
- Identify scheduling edge cases.
- Measure booking reliability.
- Validate staff adoption.

### Phase 3 — Expanded pilot
Increase organization and appointment volume only after predefined gates pass.

### Phase 4 — General availability consideration
Use pilot evidence, reliability, privacy, support, and product metrics to decide whether broader rollout is appropriate.

## Pilot selection criteria

Potential organizations should have:
- Meaningful appointment volume.
- Clear operational ownership.
- Willingness to participate in feedback.
- Defined escalation contacts.
- Scheduling workflows compatible with MVP scope.

## Launch hypothesis

If the product reduces administrative appointment friction while maintaining scheduling integrity and reliable communication, pilot organizations should demonstrate measurable improvement in defined product metrics.

This is a hypothesis to validate, not a claim of observed impact.
