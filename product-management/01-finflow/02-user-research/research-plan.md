# FinFlow — User Research Plan

> **Status:** Simulated portfolio research. Participants and findings in this package are synthetic and are not presented as real user research.

## Research Objective

Understand how consumers currently manage everyday finances, where they experience friction, and what would make a personal-finance product useful enough to revisit regularly.

## Research Questions

1. How do users currently track income and spending?
2. What makes budgeting difficult?
3. Which financial tasks require the most effort?
4. How do users identify unusual or unnecessary spending?
5. What prevents users from consistently reviewing their finances?
6. What makes users trust or distrust financial-management products?
7. Which insights would users consider actionable?
8. What would make users return to FinFlow?

## Target Participants

| Segment | Description | Planned Sample |
|---|---|---:|
| Early-career professional | 0–5 years of work experience | 4 |
| Budget-conscious professional | Actively tracks monthly spending | 3 |
| Goal-oriented saver | Saving for a defined goal | 2 |
| Finance-management beginner | Limited structured tracking | 3 |

**Simulated sample size:** 12 participants.

## Method

A mixed qualitative approach is represented:
- Semi-structured interviews
- Task walkthroughs
- Current-workflow discussion
- Concept-reaction questions

## Research Tasks

Participants are asked to describe:
- Their current tracking method
- A recent budgeting experience
- How they review transactions
- How they handle unexpected expenses
- How they monitor savings goals
- Their reaction to proposed FinFlow workflows

## Analysis Framework

Responses are grouped into:
1. Behavioral patterns
2. Pain points
3. Needs
4. Motivations
5. Trust concerns
6. Opportunities

## Research Ethics

This portfolio uses synthetic participants. No real person's financial information is included.
